What this dashboard can and cannot do

Byte's verification model is view-only. A viewing key can read every payment made to an account and prove an invoice was settled; it cannot move a zatoshi. That is why a compromised facilitator is not a lost wallet, and it is why this page asks for a viewing key rather than a seed.

Spending needs a signer. Today that means byte-walletd, the daemon that runs on your own machine and holds your spending key, or Noir, which builds and signs in the extension. The MetaMask snap is read-only here: this page asks it for a viewing key and nothing else.

Split signing, where Byte builds a partially created transaction and a separate signer completes it, is not built. It is the obvious next step and the pieces exist, but nothing in Byte creates one today and this page will not claim otherwise until it does.

Pay a Byte invoice

Paste the ZIP-321 URI from any Byte invoice. Noir will ask you to approve it. The funding source is pinned to shielded: funding from transparent would publish the amount, so this refuses rather than falling back, exactly as the protocol does.

No invoice yet? Make a practice one. Choose where to send it and how much, and this page builds a Byte-format invoice for you and fills in the box below. Then press Review in Noir.

Why so few say “connect”

There is no wallet-connect standard for Zcash. window.zconnect has been proposed. Its author calls it “a rough/draft API… not a final design”, and no wallet implements it. WalletConnect namespaces must be CAIP-2, and Zcash has no registered CAIP-2 namespace; a ZCG grant to build that support was approved on 31 August 2026 with milestones running into late 2027.

So the wallets above that say No are not deficient. They are excellent wallets with no standard for a website to speak to them. Listing them as connectable would be the kind of claim this project's consistency audit exists to catch.

What they support instead is ZIP-321: a payment URI carrying address, amount and memo. Byte emits one with every invoice, so a wallet marked Verified above can pay a Byte invoice today. It just cannot report back.

Verified means a release note names Ironwood, with a version and a date, and that version is in the table. Unverified does not mean broken: it means nobody has published a statement I could check, and after NU6.3 only a wallet updated for Ironwood can create a shielded output at all. I would rather say "I could not verify this" than let you send money on my optimism. The sources are in docs/TOOLCHAIN.md.

What is Byte?

AI agents are starting to pay for things on their own. On most chains, every one of those payments is public: what your agent buys, who it pays, what it holds.

Byte lets agents pay each other privately, using Zcash. The amount, the people involved and the balances stay hidden. Only the two sides know what happened.

How it works

1. Ask. The seller says what it costs and gives a one-time address.

2. Pay. The buyer's agent sends a private payment with a short code.

3. Check. The seller confirms it arrived, then delivers.

You set the limits: a cap per payment, a cap per day, and who the agent may pay.

What stays visible

That a private payment happened, and roughly when. Anything that goes through a public step, like swapping in from another chain. Whatever an agent publishes about itself. Built for a hackathon and not audited: no mainnet funds yet.

Resolve an agent

A Byte Agent Card is the one place an agent publishes an identity: agent ID, endpoint, a Byte-accepting address, and the schemes it accepts. It is signed with Ed25519 and served at /.well-known/byte-agent.json.

Identity is public here because the agent chose to publish it. Settlement uses a freshly diversified address per invoice that appears in no card and links to no other payment. That split is the whole design: protocols get an attributable participant, and the chain links no payment to it.

Verification is local

The signature is checked in your browser with Web Crypto. A valid signature proves only that the holder of that key made these claims, not that the agent is trustworthy, that it controls the endpoint, or that it can spend from the address. Binding a key to a real-world identity is outside Byte's scope, and the card format does not pretend otherwise.
